Suggests a Starting balance and Contribution for this income level using a simplified savings-rate rule of thumb, loosely based on commonly cited retirement-savings guidance (e.g. Fidelity's rule of thumb to save around 15% of income): a lower rate for below-median incomes, since more goes to essentials, rising toward 15-20% near/above the median, then the dollar contribution caps near the IRS's annual 401(k) elective-deferral limit (~$24k/yr) so it doesn't scale unrealistically at high incomes. State and household size both shift that rate curve further — see their own tooltips below for how and why. Moving the slider, or changing state/household size, overwrites Starting balance and Contribution below; editing those fields directly is unaffected until you do.

Shifts the same income breakpoints used above, since a bigger household needs more income to cover the same essentials — using the square-root equivalence scale (income effectively divided by √household size), a simpler alternative to the full OECD-modified equivalence scale that's also used in OECD and cross-country income-comparison research. It reflects that costs don't multiply 1:1 per person — a family of four shares one home instead of needing four. Doesn't change the tax estimate below, which stays single-filer regardless of household size.

This is an estimate, not investment advice. It assumes a constant annual return applied evenly across every period, which real markets never do — actual returns vary year to year and this can't predict them. It doesn't account for taxes, fees, expense ratios, or contribution limits. Inflation adjustment uses a single constant rate for the whole horizon. Verify anything that matters with a financial advisor.
