This is an estimate, not tax advice. Federal figures use 2026 IRS brackets and standard deductions (Rev. Proc. 2025‑32), including the federal Child Tax Credit ($2,200/child, phased out above $200,000 single/HoH or $400,000 MFJ gross income — approximated using gross income rather than a full AGI calculation). State figures use each state's published 2026 rate structure, simplified — no local/city taxes, itemized deductions, or phase‑outs beyond what's noted. State‑level dependent benefits vary enormously by state (some are credits, some are exemptions, amounts differ widely) and are not built in — enter your own state's per‑child amount in §1 if you know it; it's applied as a direct credit against state tax as an approximation. Head‑of‑household state brackets are approximated with single‑filer tables. Each person here is taxed as an individual filer (Single/HoH/MFJ) on their own combined job income; this tool does not combine two people's incomes into one joint return. A single job's "net" is that job's proportional share of its person's total tax and deductions by income share — not actual per‑employer withholding, which each employer calculates independently. Weekly/biweekly/semi‑monthly/monthly figures always divide by a fixed 52/26/24/12 — they stay consistent with each other even if "paid weeks" is reduced below 52 to model unpaid time off; hourly and daily figures reflect the rate actually earned while working.
